Idea of the project

Empowering young people to understand, prevent and respond to cyberbullying, discrimination and online hate, using participatory theatre and digital tools.

The project was inspired by the personal experiences of two young women from Bulgaria and Türkiye who faced cyberbullying and decided to turn those experiences into positive action. Their stories reflect a wider challenge across Europe, where online harassment affects emotional well-being, self-esteem, relationships and young people's confidence to take part in their communities.

For nine days in Bansko we look at bullying as something that needs collective action. Forum theatre, role play and image theatre let us explore a situation from the side of the person targeted, the bystander and the person doing it.

Project objectives

  • Raise awareness about bullying, cyberbullying, discrimination and online hate.
  • Develop empathy, emotional intelligence and resilience.
  • Strengthen young people's ability to respond to online violence.
  • Equip participants with practical digital activism and campaigning skills.

Erasmus+ is more than a semester abroad

Most people hear "Erasmus" and think of a university student spending a semester in another country. That is one part of it. Erasmus+ is the European Union's programme for education, training, youth and sport, and much of it is for young people who are not at university at all. This exchange is one example. Here are the ways in.

Ages 13–305–21 daysThis is us

Youth exchanges

Groups of young people from two or more countries live together for a week or two and work on one topic through games, workshops, theatre and discussion. No exams, no grades. Group leaders are 18 or older.

Covered: travel, food, accommodation and the activities.

Ages 18–302 weeks–12 months

Volunteering

Through the European Solidarity Corps, Erasmus+'s sister programme, you volunteer full-time on a project abroad: alone, or in a team of at least five volunteers from two or more countries.

Covered: travel, accommodation, food, insurance, language support and a small monthly allowance.

Age 18Up to 7 travel daysClosing soon

DiscoverEU

If you are 18, you can apply for a free travel pass to explore Europe, mostly by train, alone or with friends. You also get a discount card for museums, culture and local transport.

New application rounds usually open in spring and autumn.

Young people3–24 months

Youth participation projects

A group of young people runs its own project at home, on something they want to change in their community. Young people lead every stage, from the idea to the follow-up.

Covered: practical and activity costs.

Youth workers2–60 days

Training for youth workers

Training courses, seminars and study visits abroad for people who work with young people: youth workers, group leaders, volunteers at youth organisations.

Covered: travel and living costs.

School and VET

School and vocational learners

School pupils, apprentices and vocational students can spend time abroad at a partner school or doing a work placement, through their own school or training provider.

Ask your school whether it takes part.

Students

University study and traineeships

The one everyone knows: a semester or year at a partner university, or a traineeship abroad, arranged through your university's international office.

Is this for me?

Do I need to be a student?
No. Youth exchanges, volunteering and DiscoverEU do not ask what you study, or whether you study at all.
Do I need perfect English?
No. You need enough to talk with people, and you will use it every day. Plenty of participants arrive shy about their English and leave speaking it far more easily.
Who pays?
For a youth exchange, the EU grant covers your food, accommodation and activities, and refunds your travel within the project's rules. Some organisations ask a small membership fee, so ask before you apply.
I have never travelled without my family.
On a youth exchange you travel with your national team and a group leader who is 18 or older, so you are never on your own.
Can I apply by myself?
For exchanges you apply to an organisation that sends a team from your country. For volunteering and DiscoverEU you register yourself on the European Youth Portal.
What if I don't usually get chances like this?
Then you are exactly who Erasmus+ wants to reach. Projects give priority, and extra support, to young people from rural areas, with fewer resources, with disabilities, and to first-timers.
What do I get at the end?
A Youthpass certificate that describes what you learned, in a format you can show schools and employers.
How often can I go?
You can take part in more than one project, though many projects give priority to first-timers. Plenty of people start with one exchange and keep going.

Your first youth exchange, step by step

  1. Find a callFollow youth organisations in your town on social media and check the European Youth Portal. Calls usually say the topic, dates, country and age range.
  2. ApplyUsually a short form and a few lines on why the topic matters to you. Be honest; motivation matters more than experience.
  3. PrepareMeet your team online, read the infopack, and get your travel plan approved by the organisers before you buy any tickets.
  4. GoWorkshops by day, intercultural nights in the evening, and a group of new friends from across Europe.
  5. Get your YouthpassReflect on what you learned and receive your certificate on the last day.
  6. Bring it homeShare what you learned with your school, friends or town. This website is our version of that.
